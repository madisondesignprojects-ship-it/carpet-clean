Dryer vent cleaning

What gets cleaned

The whole exhaust path, not just the lint screen.

The lint filter is only the part of the dryer system you clean every load. The exhaust path behind the appliance can also collect lint and debris, especially where airflow is restricted.

Dryer connection

The accessible connection where the dryer sends exhaust into the vent system is checked as part of the service.

Vent duct

The vent path is cleaned to remove lint and other debris that can restrict airflow.

Exterior termination

The outside vent is checked to make sure the exhaust path is not obviously blocked or restricted.

Airflow check

After cleaning, airflow is checked so obvious restrictions can be identified before the job is finished.

Why it matters

Drying performance tells you a lot.

A restricted exhaust path can make a dryer take longer to do the same job. U.S. Fire Administration and NFPA guidance also emphasize regular lint and vent maintenance because accumulated lint can contribute to dryer-fire risk.

Longer dry times

Clothes taking much longer than usual can be a sign that airflow through the exhaust path is restricted.

Extra heat

An unusually hot laundry area or dryer can be a reason to have the venting checked.

Lint buildup

Lint around the vent, back of the dryer or exhaust area is a reason to pay attention to the venting system.

The cleaning process

What happens during a dryer-vent cleaning.

  1. Inspect

    We review the dryer connection, visible venting and exterior termination to understand the setup.

  2. Access

    The dryer and vent connection are accessed safely as needed for the cleaning.

  3. Remove buildup

    Specialized tools are used to loosen and remove lint and debris from the vent path.

  4. Check airflow

    We finish by checking the exhaust path and pointing out any restriction, damage or access issue we can identify.

What homeowners should know

Professional cleaning does not replace basic dryer maintenance.

The lint filter still needs to be cleaned before and after use. The U.S. Fire Administration also recommends checking the venting system for damage, crushing or restriction and making sure the outdoor vent opens while the dryer runs.

  • Clean the lint filter before and after each cycle.
  • Keep the area around the dryer clear of things that can burn.
  • Check for obvious crushing, damage or restriction in the visible venting.
  • Keep the exterior termination free of blockage.
Maintenance

A clean vent is about keeping the exhaust path open.

For many homes, annual vent cleaning is a practical maintenance interval. Heavy laundry use, long vent runs or other conditions may call for more frequent attention.

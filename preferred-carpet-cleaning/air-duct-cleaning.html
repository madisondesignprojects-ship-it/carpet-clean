Air duct cleaning

What it means

Air duct cleaning is really HVAC-system cleaning.

Industry guidance treats the job as more than cleaning the openings you can see. Depending on the system and the scope, cleaning can involve supply and return ducts, registers and grilles, plenums and other accessible HVAC components.

  • Supply air ducts
  • Return air ducts and grilles
  • Registers, diffusers and vent covers
  • Accessible plenums and system components
  • Other components included in the agreed scope of work
Source removal

How a thorough duct cleaning is supposed to work.

The basic idea is simple: loosen the material that has collected inside the system, keep that material contained, and remove it rather than sending it back into the home.

  1. Inspect

    The system is reviewed first so the technician can understand access, components and the scope of the work.

  2. Contain

    The cleaning setup is arranged to keep loosened debris from spreading into the living space.

  3. Agitate

    Specialized brushes, air tools or contact vacuuming can loosen material from interior surfaces.

  4. Collect

    Vacuum collection removes the loosened debris so the system can be inspected after cleaning.

When it can make sense

There are better reasons than “it’s been a while.”

EPA does not recommend routine air-duct cleaning simply because a certain number of years have passed. Cleaning can make more sense when there is substantial visible contamination, mold growth in appropriate components, pests or vermin, or significant deposits of dust and debris.

  • Visible, substantial dust or debris inside the system
  • Suspected contamination that can be shown and explained
  • Visible mold growth on appropriate hard-surface components
  • Pests or vermin in the duct system
  • Post-renovation conditions that left the HVAC system contaminated
What customers should know

A good duct-cleaning quote should be specific.

Before work begins, you should understand what the company is cleaning, what parts of the system are included, how access will be handled and how debris will be contained.

Useful questions to ask

  • Which HVAC components are included in the quoted service?
  • How will dust and debris be contained during cleaning?
  • Will the company show me the condition of the system or the results?
  • Are any chemical treatments being proposed, and why?
A practical expectation

Cleaner components. A system you can actually see and understand.

The most useful result of a professional cleaning is knowing what was cleaned, what was found and whether anything needs attention beyond the cleaning itself.

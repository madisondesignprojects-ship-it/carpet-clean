Upholstery cleaning

What gets cleaned

More than a quick pass over the cushions.

Professional upholstery cleaning is designed to remove the dirt and residue that collect on the surface and within the fabric. What can be safely cleaned depends on the furniture, material and care instructions.

Sofas

Whole-sofa cleaning can address arms, cushions, backs and other accessible upholstered surfaces.

Chairs & recliners

Everyday use leaves oils, dust and spots where people sit, lean and rest their arms.

Dining & accent pieces

Dining chairs, ottomans and other upholstered pieces can often be evaluated for the right cleaning approach.

Fabric matters

The tag on the furniture is important.

Upholstery is not one material. Care labels can indicate whether a fabric is intended for water-based cleaning, solvent-based cleaning, either approach, or no cleaning solution at all. The safest approach is to identify the material and follow the manufacturer's care guidance before choosing a method.

W

Water-based cleaning is appropriate when the manufacturer's care code allows it.

S

Only solvent-based cleaning is recommended for this code; the wrong method can damage the fabric.

WS

Either water- or solvent-based cleaning may be acceptable, depending on the material and instructions.

X

Do not use cleaning solutions. Vacuuming or a specialty professional service may be required.

What affects the result?

  • Fabric type and construction
  • Colorfastness and existing wear
  • Age and type of stains
  • Previous cleaning or spot-treatment attempts
  • How much soil has built up over time
The cleaning process

A careful upholstery job is built around the fabric.

  1. Inspect

    We look for the care information, identify the material and check for stains, wear, damage or other conditions that affect the cleaning plan.

  2. Prepare

    Loose debris and problem areas are addressed before the main cleaning step, with care taken around delicate parts of the furniture.

  3. Clean

    The cleaning approach is selected for the upholstery material and the condition of the piece rather than using one method for everything.

  4. Finish

    We check the result with you and explain drying time and any care steps you should follow after service.

Stains and odors

Cleaning can improve a piece without pretending it can reverse damage.

Fresh spills are usually easier to address than old, set-in staining. Some discoloration is permanent because the fiber or dye has been changed. Previous spot-treatment products can also change how a stain responds.

  • Oil, body soil and everyday grime
  • Food and drink spills
  • Pet-related spots and odors
  • Darkened arms, seats and headrests
  • Stains that need a professional assessment before treatment
After cleaning

Give the fabric time to dry before putting it back to work.

Drying time varies by fabric, cleaning method, moisture level, airflow and room conditions. Upholstery should be allowed to dry fully before heavy use, and we’ll explain what to expect before we leave.
